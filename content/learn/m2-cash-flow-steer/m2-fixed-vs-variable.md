*The fixed/variable distinction below is adapted from Wikipedia's "Fixed cost" article (CC BY-SA 4.0), paraphrased rather than quoted, and applied here to household spending rather than business accounting.*

A **fixed cost** stays the same each period regardless of what you do that month — rent, a car
payment, insurance. A **variable cost** changes with your choices — groceries, dining out,
shopping. The distinction matters for exactly one reason: it tells you where a budget decision this
month can actually change the outcome. You can decide to spend less on dining out tonight. You
cannot decide to owe less rent tonight.

That's why "cut your spending" as generic advice so often fails — it doesn't say which spending.

furthest off track are:

absolute terms, but because they're the ones currently drifting the most from what you intended.

:::worked
Rent is $1,800 fixed. Groceries budgeted at $500 came in at $640. The rent number isn't a lever this
month — it's contractual. The $140 grocery overage is: it's the direct result of choices made this
month, and it's the one that responds to attention.
:::

:::check
**Question:** Why focus budget attention on variable costs rather than fixed ones?
- A. Variable costs are always larger
- B. Fixed costs are contractual and don't respond to a monthly decision; variable costs do
- C. Fixed costs don't appear in the ledger
**Correct answer:** B — this month's choices can only change spending that isn't already locked in by contract.
:::
