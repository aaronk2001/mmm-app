Finding a controllable outflow and setting a budget on it is only half the win — the other half is
making the improvement durable instead of a one-month blip that quietly reverts. Two mechanics do
most of that work: rollover, so unspent room in a category doesn't just vanish and remove the
incentive to underspend, and a backup, so the progress you've made is never one bad sync away from
being lost.

Your monthly surplus is currently **{{monthly_surplus.formatted}}**, and your savings rate is
**{{savings_rate.formatted}}**. Every lesson in M2 was aimed at moving those two numbers — a
controlled outflow becomes a larger surplus, and a larger surplus, kept rather than re-absorbed
into spending, becomes a higher savings rate. That's the whole mechanism, end to end: find the
lever, pull it, then make the result stick instead of drifting back.

:::worked
A household cuts $150/month in dining out via a category budget. Without rollover, the unspent $150
just resets to zero next month with no record it ever existed. With rollover, it accumulates
visibly — turning one month's discipline into a running total that reinforces itself instead of
disappearing.
:::

:::

:::check
**Question:** What problem does budget rollover specifically solve?
- A. It automatically raises your income
- B. It prevents unspent room in a category from resetting to zero, so improvements accumulate instead of disappearing
- C. It removes the need to categorize transactions
**Correct answer:** B — without rollover, a good month and an average month look identical next period, which removes the incentive to keep the improvement.
:::

:::apply
**Apply — Enable rollover.** Turn on rollover so the effort from this module's budget changes
compounds instead of resetting every month.
:::
