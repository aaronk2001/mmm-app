The FDIC's Money Smart curriculum treats an emergency fund less like a one-time goal and more like
a habit: a recurring transfer, sized to what you can actually sustain, that runs whether or not you
remember to think about it that month. The "pay yourself first" framing exists for a reason — money
that has to be manually moved competes with every other spending decision in your week and
routinely loses.

You have **{{monthly_surplus.formatted}}** in monthly surplus to work with. Applied consistently
toward the gap between your current **{{ef_months_covered.formatted}} months covered** and your
**{{ef_target.formatted}}** target, that surplus is what actually closes the distance — not a lump
sum you're waiting to have, but a repeating contribution you commit to now.

:::worked
A household with a $300/mo surplus and a $3,600 gap to their target closes it in 12 months if the
full surplus is automated into savings the day it lands — and in "eventually, maybe" if it's left
to whatever's unspent at the end of each month, which is rarely the same amount twice.
:::

emergency fund starting this month, roughly how many months would it take to close your gap to
{{ef_target.formatted}}? ______________ (rough math is fine).
:::

:::check
**Question:** Why does Money Smart favor an automated recurring transfer over a manual one?
- A. Automated transfers earn a higher interest rate
- B. A recurring transfer happens regardless of that month's competing spending decisions, so it's more reliable
- C. Manual transfers are illegal in most states
**Correct answer:** B — removing the monthly decision point is what makes the contribution consistent.
:::

:::apply
**Apply — Add a recurring transfer.** Set up a recurring contribution toward your emergency fund,
sized against your {{monthly_surplus.formatted}} surplus, so the gap to {{ef_target.formatted}} closes automatically each month.
:::

:::
