The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau frames emergency savings around one specific job: covering
an unplanned, necessary expense — a car repair, a medical bill, a lost paycheck — without reaching
for high-interest debt to do it. That's a narrower purpose than "savings" in general. Money you're
setting aside for a vacation or a down payment is saving, but it isn't your emergency fund, because
you'd be reluctant to raid it the moment a real emergency showed up.

The CFPB's practical guidance doesn't require you to hit a big number before the fund starts
working: even a small starter buffer measurably cuts how often people turn to credit cards or
payday loans for a surprise expense. Progress before completion still counts.

:::worked
Someone with $1,200 saved and $3,000/mo in expenses has 0.4 months covered. A $500 car repair
doesn't need to become a credit-card balance for them — their starter fund absorbs it directly, even
though they're nowhere near a full target yet.
:::
