This app is a ledger, not an advisor. It does not predict, recommend a fund, or tell you what to
do with your paycheck. It records what actually happened to your money, then computes numbers —
runway, burn, cash flow — directly from those records so you're reasoning from your own history
instead of a rule of thumb that was written for someone else's life.

Everything downstream of that ledger is arithmetic. Runway is your liquid cash divided by what you
spend. Your emergency-fund gap is your target minus what you've saved. There's no model between
your numbers and the screen — which is also why this Learn section exists: the app can compute a
figure, but it can't explain why the figure matters. That's what the next fourteen lessons do.

Three places to know before anything else:

- **Overview** — the front page. Runway, cash flow, and net worth in one screen.
- **Cash Flow** — every transaction and recurring bill that makes up your ledger.
- **Learn** (here) — short lessons that use the numbers from the other two.

:::worked
Think of the ledger like a bank statement that never resets. A bank statement shows one month;
this ledger keeps every month, so a number like "runway" isn't a snapshot — it's the current answer
to "at my recent pace, how long does my liquid cash last?", recomputed every time you open the app.
:::

:::faded
Your turn — without opening another tab, guess: if the app disappeared tomorrow and you only had
your bank's mobile app, could you answer "how long does my cash last at my current spending" in
under a minute? _____ (yes / no). If no, that's exactly the gap this app closes.
:::

:::check
**Question:** What does this app compute your numbers from?
- A. Predictions about the economy
- B. Your own recorded transactions and recurring bills
- C. A generic budget template
**Correct answer:** B — every figure the app shows traces back to something you actually recorded, not a template or a forecast.
:::

:::
